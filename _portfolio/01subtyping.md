## Overview

Dementia is biologically heterogeneous, and broad diagnostic labels can hide meaningful differences in mechanisms, progression and treatment response. This project develops exploratory workflows for integrating genomic, multi-omic and longitudinal cohort data to identify candidate dementia subtypes.

The work is research and hypothesis generation. It is not a deployed clinical tool.

## My Contribution

- Developed the analytical framing for patient stratification using multi-omic and clinical data.
- Worked on integrating WGS, transcriptomic, methylation, proteomic, longitudinal clinical and derived cohort information into analysis-ready structures.
- Built preprocessing and evaluation workflows for high-dimensional biomedical data.
- Applied clustering, semi-supervised learning and dimensionality reduction approaches where appropriate to the data and study aims.
- Planned validation around clinical endpoints and pathway-oriented biological interpretation.

## Why It Matters

The project shows how I approach complex biomedical data: start with a careful cohort and data-readiness question, build reproducible analysis steps, and keep interpretation tied to biology rather than treating clustering as a black box.
